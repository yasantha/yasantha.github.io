Rain, white noise or brown noise? Choosing focus sounds for studying

Updated 26 October 2026 · 6 min read

Some people need silence to concentrate. Many others find silence harder: every footstep, notification or conversation in the next room pulls their attention away. A steady background sound covers those little noises, so your brain stops reacting to each one.

The right sound is personal, and it can change with the task. This guide explains the common focus sounds in plain language and how to find a mix that works for you.

Why steady sounds help

Sudden sounds grab attention because they're unpredictable. A constant, even sound in the background makes those sudden sounds less noticeable. It's the same reason a fan or the rain can help some people sleep. You're not adding something interesting to listen to; you're adding something boring that hides the interesting distractions.

That's also why music with lyrics often works badly for reading or writing. Words compete with the words you're trying to process.

The main focus sounds, compared

SoundWhat it sounds likeOften good for
White noiseBright hiss, like TV static or a hairdryerCovering voices and sharp noises in a busy room
Pink noiseSofter than white, like steady rain on a roofLong reading sessions when white noise feels harsh
Brown noiseDeep rumble, like a waterfall or strong windPeople who find higher sounds irritating; calm, heavy focus
RainNatural, slightly uneven patterStudying and writing; feels cosy rather than technical
Ocean wavesSlow rise and fallRelaxed work, light admin, reading
FireplaceSoft crackle with occasional popsEvening study; adds warmth to a quiet room
Night cricketsGentle rhythmic chirpingLate sessions; a calm, outdoor feel

The "colours" of noise describe how the sound energy is spread from high to low pitches. You don't need the physics. Just remember: white is bright, pink is softer, brown is deep.

How to choose: a 3-day experiment

Everyone reacts differently, so test instead of guessing:

  1. Day 1: Rain only, at a low volume, for every session.
  2. Day 2: Brown noise only.
  3. Day 3: Your favourite of the two, plus one other layer such as ocean waves or a fireplace.

After each day, notice how many sessions you finished and how easy it was to start the next one. The sound that makes starting easiest is the right one, even if another one "should" be better.

Layering sounds

A single sound can start to feel monotonous. Layering two or three gives you a richer background that still stays out of the way. Some combinations to try:

  • Rain + brown noise: rain on top for texture, brown noise underneath to cover voices. A good all-rounder for shared homes.
  • Fireplace + night crickets: a calm evening mix for reading or journaling.
  • Ocean waves + pink noise: soft and slow, for long reading sessions.

Keep the extra layers quieter than the main sound. If you start noticing the sounds themselves, turn them down.

Volume and comfort

  • Keep the volume low: just loud enough to cover the distractions, not louder.
  • If you use headphones for hours, take them off during breaks to give your ears a rest.
  • If a sound makes you sleepy, switch to something brighter for afternoon sessions.

Match the sound to the task

  • Memorising and flashcards: a simple single sound such as pink noise or rain.
  • Writing: rain or brown noise; avoid anything with words.
  • Maths and problem solving: brown noise, or silence if you prefer.
  • Admin and email: ocean waves or a fireplace; something lighter.

The best focus sound is the one that makes you forget it's playing. Start simple, test for a few days, and keep the mix that helps you finish the most sessions.
